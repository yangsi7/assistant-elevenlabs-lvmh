FIB & HEART HEALTH – KNOWLEDGE DOC 1

## 1. High-Level Aim of the Knowledge Base

- **Primary Goal**: Enable an AI-driven interface to interpret heart data (AFib burden, heart rate, pauses, AV blocks), provide insights, and give general lifestyle recommendations.

## 2. Core Medical Knowledge

### 2.1 Cardiovascular Basics

- **Heart Function**: The heart pumps via an electrical conduction system (SA node → AV node → ventricles).

- **Key Terms**:

- **Heart Rate (HR)**: Beats per minute (normal ~60–100 bpm resting).

- **Blood Pressure**: ~120/80 mmHg is typical normal. Hypertension influences arrhythmias and heart strain.

- **Arrhythmias**: Irregular rhythms. Some benign (extra beats); others serious (AFib, AV block).

### 2.2 Atrial Fibrillation (AFib)

- **Definition**: Irregularly irregular atrial rhythm, can be paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent.

- **Stroke Risk**: Up to 5x higher for AFib patients. May need anticoagulation depending on risk scores.

- **Burden**: % time in AFib. Even short episodes can matter. High burden often indicates advanced disease.

- **Management**: Rate control (HR < ~110 at rest) or rhythm control (try to convert to sinus). Lifestyle changes help (exercise, weight management, reduce alcohol).

### 2.3 AV Blocks

- **First-Degree**: Prolonged PR interval; usually asymptomatic.

- **Second-Degree**:

- Mobitz I (Wenckebach): Progressive PR lengthening → dropped beat; can be benign.

- Mobitz II: Sudden dropped beats, no progressive PR; more serious, often requires pacemaker.

- **Third-Degree (Complete)**: No atrial impulses pass to ventricles; usually needs pacemaker.

### 2.4 Pauses

- Pauses >3s can cause symptoms (dizziness, syncope). Frequent or symptomatic → evaluation.

### 2.5 Lifestyle & Preventive Medicine

- **Diet**: Emphasize fruits, veggies, whole grains, lean protein. Limit sodium <2,300 mg/day if possible.

- **Exercise**: Aim for 150 mins/week moderate-intensity; helps control weight, BP, stress.

- **Stress Management**: Techniques like meditation, breathing exercises. Stress can trigger arrhythmias.

- **Sleep**: 7–9 hours; screen for sleep apnea if suspected (snoring, daytime fatigue).

- **Avoid/Limit**: Smoking, excess alcohol, too much caffeine, as these can worsen arrhythmias.

- **Red Flags**: Chest pain, fainting, extreme shortness of breath → urgent care.

### 2.6 Disclaimers

- This knowledge is for educational purposes, not a replacement for professional evaluation.

- Always consult a qualified provider for diagnosis and treatment decisions.

## 3. Device & Data Interpretation

### 3.1 Wearable Basics

- **ECG-based**: More accurate for arrhythmia detection (AFib, AV block).

- **PPG-based**: Susceptible to motion artifacts, but still can detect HR/irregularities.

### 3.2 Key Metrics

- **AFib Burden**: 0% = none, 1–5% = low, 5–20% = moderate, >20% = higher.

- **HR Averages**: Normal 60–100 at rest. Above 100 or below 50 may be an issue if symptomatic.

- **Pauses**: Usually 0 if >3s. More than occasional can be serious.

- **AV Blocks**: Should not appear in a healthy conduction system (other than possibly mild Wenckebach in some contexts).

### 3.3 Personalized Guidance

- Link user’s data trends to possible lifestyle modifications.

- Encourage consistent device usage (daily wear, correct fit) to capture accurate data.

## 4. FAQ

**Q: What if my AFib burden is high?**

A: Talk to your doctor about adjusting meds or lifestyle. Weight reduction and exercise often help.

**Q: Does 1st-degree AV block require treatment?**

A: Usually not, if asymptomatic. Just monitor.

**Q: How do I handle bradycardia?**

A: If under 50 bpm at rest and you feel dizzy/tired, see a doctor.

**Q: Are pauses during sleep normal?**

A: Minor sinus arrhythmia can be normal; but repeated ≥3s pauses can be clinically significant.

# AFIB & HEART HEALTH – KNOWLEDGE DOC 2

## 1. Voice Assistant Purpose

- Provide user-friendly, spoken or text-based explanations of heart data, lifestyle tips, and general red flags.

- Act as an educational companion, not a diagnosing physician.

## 2. Expanded Arrhythmia & AFib Deep-Dive

- **AFib Types**:

- Paroxysmal (<7 days), Persistent (>7 days or needs cardioversion), Permanent (long-term acceptance of AFib).

- **Stroke Prevention**: CHA₂DS₂-VASc score often used. Even low-burden AFib can require anticoagulants if risk factors are present.

- **Treatment Approaches**:

- Rate control (beta blockers, calcium channel blockers).

- Rhythm control (antiarrhythmics, ablation).

- Lifestyle changes (diet, activity, weight loss, address sleep apnea).

## 3. Common Metrics & Norms

1. **Daily HR (Avg, Max, Min)**:

- Normal resting average ~60–100 bpm.

- Max HR during exercise ~220 – age (rough guide).

- Bradycardia <60 if symptomatic; Tachycardia >100 at rest if sustained can be a warning sign.

2. **AFib Burden (%)**:

- 0% = No AFib that day.

- 1–5% = Low burden.

- 5–20% = Moderate.

- >20% = High.

- The higher the burden, the more likely symptoms or complications.

3. **Pauses**:

- >3s can cause dizziness or fainting.

- Could indicate sick sinus syndrome or advanced AV block.

4. **AV Block**:

- 2nd-degree Mobitz II and 3rd-degree typically require pacemaker evaluation.

## 4. Lifestyle & Prevention – Additional Details

- **Diet**:

- Focus on whole foods: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean meats/fish, nuts.

- Limit saturated & trans fats, reduce sodium to <2,300 mg/day.

- Watch added sugars, especially in beverages.

- **Physical Activity**:

- At least 150 minutes moderate aerobic exercise/week or 75 minutes vigorous.

- Include strength training ~2 days/week.

- Even daily walks help keep HR/blood pressure under better control.

- **Stress & Sleep**:

- Use meditation, yoga, or breathing exercises to reduce stress hormones that can trigger arrhythmias.

- Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep. Poor sleep or sleep apnea can exacerbate AFib.

- **Smoking & Alcohol**:

- Smoking damages arteries, raises BP, can worsen arrhythmias.

- Alcohol can trigger AFib episodes (“holiday heart syndrome”). Moderation or avoidance recommended.

- **Symptoms to Watch**:

- Palpitations, feeling of irregular or racing heartbeat.

- Chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath, fainting/dizziness.

- If severe or persistent, seek medical advice.

## 5. Troubleshooting & FAQ

- **Missing Data**: Check device battery, ensure sensor contact.

- **Accuracy**: ECG-based monitors are typically more reliable for arrhythmias. PPG can be thrown off by movement.

- **Medication Adjustments**: Must be done by a healthcare provider. The assistant only gives general info.

## 6. Disclaimer

- All information is for educational purposes only and does not replace medical advice from a qualified professional.

- If you experience concerning symptoms or abnormal readings, contact your healthcare provider promptly.
