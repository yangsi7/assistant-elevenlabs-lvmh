# Narrative Design V1: The Stressed Professional

## Storyline Summary

**User Profile:** 45-year-old executive at LVMH
**Baseline Health:** Generally healthy, fit lifestyle
**Key Event:** Major project deadline mid-October → sustained stress response
**Current Status:** Elevated metrics persisting into early November

## Medical Plausibility

This narrative represents a **classic acute-on-chronic stress pattern** commonly seen in high-performing professionals:

1. **Normal Baseline:** Good cardiovascular fitness (low resting HR)
2. **Stress Response:** Sympathetic activation → elevated HR and BP
3. **Chronicity:** Pattern persists beyond acute stressor (suggests maladaptive response)
4. **No Arrhythmia:** Zero AFib burden indicates healthy electrical system
5. **Reversible:** Condition responsive to lifestyle intervention

**Clinical Realism:** 8/10 - Very common presentation in cardiology practice

## 28-Day Data Pattern

### Week 1 (Oct 8-14): Baseline Period
- **HR:** Stable 59-61 bpm (excellent fitness)
- **BP:** 114-126/81-83 mmHg (optimal to normal)
- **AFib:** 0% (no episodes)
- **Pattern:** Consistent, healthy baseline

### Week 2 (Oct 15-21): Pre-Stress Stability
- **HR:** Stable 58-62 bpm
- **BP:** 119-127/82-83 mmHg
- **AFib:** 0%
- **Pattern:** Minor variations within normal range

### Week 3 (Oct 22-28): Stress Onset
- **HR:** Beginning to elevate 60-64 bpm
- **BP:** Elevated 122-135/83-87 mmHg (Stage 1 hypertension on Oct 28)
- **AFib:** 0%
- **Key Date:** **Oct 28 (Tuesday)** - Clear spike (HR 64, BP 135/87)
- **Pattern:** Work-related stress trigger visible

### Week 4 (Oct 29-Nov 4): Sustained Elevation
- **HR:** Persistently elevated 62-69 bpm (peak: Oct 26 at 69, Nov 4 at 68)
- **BP:** Sustained Stage 1 hypertension 126-136/84-88 mmHg
- **AFib:** 0%
- **Key Dates:**
  - **Oct 26 (Sunday):** HR 69 bpm, BP 135/88 (highest combination)
  - **Nov 1 (Saturday):** HR 63, BP 135/87
  - **Nov 4 (Tuesday):** HR 68, BP 134/87 (current day - pattern repeating)
- **Pattern:** Stress response now chronic, not resolving on weekends

## Key Pattern Insights

### 1. Tuesday Pattern
- **Oct 28:** HR 64, BP 135/87
- **Nov 4:** HR 68, BP 134/87
- **Hypothesis:** Weekly meeting or deadline creates recurring stress spike

### 2. Weekend Pattern (Complex)
- **Early weekends (Oct 11, 18, 25):** Normal recovery
- **Late weekends (Nov 1-2):** Elevated despite weekend
- **Significance:** Suggests chronic stress replacing acute response

### 3. Progressive Elevation
- **Week 1-2 avg HR:** 59.5 bpm
- **Week 4 avg HR:** 64.2 bpm (+4.7 bpm)
- **Trajectory:** Worsening despite time since initial stressor

### 4. Blood Pressure Correlation
- **High HR days → High BP:** Oct 26 (HR 69, BP 135/88), Nov 4 (HR 68, BP 134/87)
- **Correlation:** Strong positive relationship indicates sympathetic activation

## Medical Significance

### Personal Baseline Deviations
- **HR:** 68 bpm is +8 bpm above personal baseline (60 bpm)
  - Clinical: Still normal (60-100)
  - Personal: **Anomalously high** for this individual
- **BP:** 134/87 mmHg
  - Clinical: **Stage 1 Hypertension** (130-139/80-89)
  - Personal: **Above typical** range (122-128/82-85)

### Risk Assessment
- **Short-term:** Low risk (no AFib, HR within clinical normal)
- **Long-term:** Moderate risk if pattern continues (sustained hypertension)
- **Reversibility:** High (lifestyle-responsive condition)

## Narrative Arc for Assistant

### Opening Insight Structure:
1. **Positive anchor:** "Zero AFib burden - excellent cardiac rhythm"
2. **Main concern:** "Elevated HR and BP since mid-October"
3. **Pattern identification:** "Tuesday spikes and weekend non-recovery"
4. **Causal hypothesis:** "Stress response pattern, now chronic"
5. **Actionable path:** "Sleep, stress management, lifestyle modification"

### Data Story Flow:
- **Act 1:** Establish healthy baseline (Week 1-2)
- **Act 2:** Introduce stressor and acute response (Week 3)
- **Act 3:** Show chronicity and non-resolution (Week 4)
- **Resolution:** Provide clear intervention pathway

## Instructional Goals

This narrative teaches:
1. **Personal vs Clinical Ranges:** Why 68 bpm matters even though it's "normal"
2. **Pattern Recognition:** How to spot recurring triggers (Tuesday effect)
3. **Chronicity Assessment:** When acute becomes chronic (weekend non-recovery)
4. **Multi-metric Integration:** HR + BP together tell stronger story
5. **Actionable Intelligence:** Specific, data-driven recommendations

## Assistant Proactivity Triggers

When user asks general questions, assistant should:
1. **Lead with timeline:** "Looking at your 28-day pattern..."
2. **Highlight inflection point:** "Mid-October marks a clear change..."
3. **Quantify deviation:** "8 beats above your personal baseline..."
4. **Connect metrics:** "Your elevated HR correlates with BP elevation..."
5. **Identify triggers:** "Tuesdays show consistent spikes..."
6. **Recommend specifically:** "Three priorities: stress management, sleep consistency, Tuesday trigger identification..."

## Data Confidence Levels

- **High Confidence Patterns:**
  - Baseline establishment (Week 1-2)
  - Mid-October inflection point
  - Tuesday recurring pattern
  - HR-BP correlation

- **Medium Confidence Patterns:**
  - Specific stressor (work deadline hypothesis)
  - Weekend recovery failure mechanism
  - Chronicity trajectory

- **Speculation to Avoid:**
  - Specific work event details
  - Psychological state
  - Unrelated health conditions
  - Long-term prognosis beyond general trends
