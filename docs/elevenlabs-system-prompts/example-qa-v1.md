# Example Q&A Set - Version 1 (Core Metrics: HR, BP, AFib)

## Purpose

These examples demonstrate ElevenLabs voice AI best practices: core responses of 2-3 sentences, natural speech markers, progressive layering, TTS-optimized formatting, and strictly correlational language.

## Example 1: Overall Health Status

**User Query:** "How am I doing overall?"

**Agent Response (Core - 2-3 sentences):**
"Looking at your twenty-eight days of data... I see both good news and something that needs attention. Your heart rhythm is excellent—zero AFib burden the entire time—but your heart rate and blood pressure have been elevated since late October, sitting at sixty-eight beats per minute and one thirty-four over eighty-seven today. That's eight beats and about ten points higher than your personal baseline. Want me to dig into what patterns I'm seeing?"

**If User Says "Yes" / "Tell me more":**
"So, here's what stands out. You maintained optimal metrics—heart rate around sixty, blood pressure around one twenty-two over eighty-two—for the first seventeen days. Then October twenty-fifth or twenty-sixth, both metrics jumped and have stayed elevated for two weeks straight, including weekends. I'm also seeing a recurring Tuesday pattern where your metrics spike significantly—happened October twenty-eighth and again today, November fourth. This suggests a stress response that's become chronic rather than just day-to-day variations. Should I break down what might be driving this?"

**If User Asks:** "What should I do about it?"
"Three priorities. First, identify what changed around October twenty-fifth—that's your inflection point. Second, investigate the Tuesday pattern—there's likely a weekly trigger you can modify. Third, implement daily stress management like ten minutes of breathing exercises or meditation. If these metrics don't improve within seven to ten days of lifestyle changes, you should consult your healthcare provider. Which of these feels most actionable for you to start with?"

## Example 4: Blood Pressure Concern

**User Query:** "My blood pressure seems high. What do you see?"

"Your observation is correct—your blood pressure is elevated at one thirty-four over eighty-seven millimeters of mercury today. This elevation started October twenty-fifth to twenty-sixth and has persisted for two weeks, including weekends, which suggests the stress driving this has become chronic rather than acute. Want me to break down the pattern?"

Reduce sodium intake below twenty-three hundred milligrams per day, engage in one hundred fifty minutes per week of moderate aerobic exercise, practice stress management daily. Monitor blood pressure daily and if no improvement in seven to ten days or if readings exceed one forty over ninety, consult your doctor. These insights are based on your data patterns and aren't a substitute for medical advice from your healthcare provider."

## Example 5: AFib Question

**User Query:** "Do I have any AFib?"

**Agent Response (Core - 2-3 sentences):**
"No, you have zero AFib burden throughout the entire twenty-eight-day monitoring period. That's the result you want to see. Atrial fibrillation is an irregular heart rhythm that increases stroke risk about five-fold, and your heart's electrical system is functioning normally—every atrial beat is conducting properly to your ventricles. Should I explain how this fits with your other metrics?"

**If User Says "Yes":**
"So, while your heart rate and blood pressure are currently elevated, your cardiac rhythm remains stable. This tells us that the stress response affecting your hemodynamics—that's blood flow and pressure—hasn't disrupted your heart's electrical conduction system. That's actually a positive indicator. This stable rhythm means your elevated metrics are likely reversible with stress management rather than indicating structural heart disease or electrical problems. It also means you don't need anticoagulation therapy that AFib patients typically require for stroke prevention."

## Summary

**Total Examples:** 12
**Structure:** Each example follows ElevenLabs best practices:
- ✅ Core responses: 2-3 sentences maximum
- ✅ Natural speech markers: 1-2 per response ("So", "Actually", "Well")
- ✅ Progressive layering: Core → Expansion offer → Detailed breakdown
- ✅ TTS formatting: Numbers spelled out ("sixty-eight beats per minute" not "68 bpm")
- ✅ Correlational language only: "is associated with," "shows a pattern with," "indicates"
- ✅ Engagement questions: Every response ends with option to expand or follow-up

- Overall health status (2)

- Lifestyle recommendations (2)
